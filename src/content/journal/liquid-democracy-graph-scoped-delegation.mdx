[Liquid Democracy](/projects/liquid-democracy) is delivered. It's my answer to
Mission 10 of the intuition.box DAO: a proof of concept of liquid democracy, live on
Intuition testnet. Built solo, and the most careful thing I've shipped so far.

## The problem

In a DAO, delegating your vote is all or nothing. You hand your voice to someone
and they vote on everything: treasury, protocol parameters, community events.
Nobody is competent everywhere.

What you'd actually want to say is: *"I trust you with my vote on the treasury.
Not on the rest."*

The catch is that "the treasury" isn't an address or a number. It's a
**domain**, with sub-parts that keep moving: grants, then builder grants, then
micro-grants, and a new programme every quarter. A classic system would list the
allowed domains at signing time, and every new programme would mean re-signing
every delegation.

## The idea

The hierarchy already exists, in the Intuition graph, where the DAO can describe
its own organisation as triples: `grants, is a sub-domain of, treasury`.

So the delegation doesn't carry a list. It carries a **question**: *is this
proposal's domain under `treasury` in the graph?* Add a branch to the graph and
every delegation already signed covers it immediately. No redeploy, no new
signature. That's the core of the demo: mint a domain live, and a delegation
signed before the meeting authorizes a vote on it.

Under the hood, each delegation is an [ERC-7710](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-7710)
delegation (MetaMask's Delegation Framework) with one custom caveat,
`GraphDomainScope`.

## The storage fact that shaped everything

My first instinct was to have the contract walk the graph up from `micro-grants`
to `treasury`. That turned out to be not expensive but **impossible**. The
MultiVault stores triples keyed by `hash(subject, predicate, object)`: a
contract can check that an edge it already knows exists, but it can never ask
"what are the children of this node?". There's no reverse index.

So the work is split, the same way a Merkle proof is. The browser finds the path
and attaches it to the vote as a **witness**. The contract doesn't believe it: it
recomputes every hop against live chain state, for about 14k gas each.

## Existence is not attestation

On Intuition, anyone can mint any triple for a fraction of a cent. If the
contract only checked that an edge exists, a delegate trusted with `treasury`
could publish `events, is a sub-domain of, treasury` and widen their own
mandate for almost nothing.

So an edge only counts if the curator has **staked** on it. Writing stays free
and open; it just doesn't authorize anything anymore. And the contract checks
the vault type too: Intuition lets you stake on the *counter*-triple ("no, A is
not part of B"), and the convenient `isTriple` helper answers yes for both.
I almost used it.

## What I didn't have to write

The part I'm happiest about is code that doesn't exist. Revocation comes from
the standard. Narrowing too: if A delegates `treasury` to B and B only passes
`grants` to C, C can't vote on `payroll`, and no line anywhere forbids it. The
framework runs every caveat in the chain and requires all of them to pass, so
the intersection does the narrowing on its own.

## Any DAO on OpenZeppelin can plug it in

Most on-chain DAOs run some version of **OpenZeppelin's Governor**. So the real
question was never "does it work on my governor?" but "does it work on theirs?".
I started on a governor I wrote myself, then moved the whole app onto
OpenZeppelin's, to take that objection off the table.

It works, and without forking anything. `Governor`, `GovernorSettings`,
`GovernorCountingSimple` and `GovernorVotes` are inherited exactly as published.
The integration is three small pieces sitting beside them:

- **`DomainVotes`** overrides `_getVotes`, the hook OpenZeppelin marks `virtual`
  precisely so a counting scheme can be swapped in. The token still says *how
  much* weight an account holds; the register says *who may use it, and where*.
- **`castVoteFor`**, because an ERC-7710 redemption executes from the
  delegator's account while OpenZeppelin reads the caller. Without it, a vote B
  casts with A's voice would be recorded as A's. It hands straight off to
  OpenZeppelin's own `_castVote`, so the tally, the double-vote guard and the
  events stay the audited ones.
- **`ProposalDomains`**, because `propose` has no field for a domain, and adding
  one would mean forking audited code. The domain lives in a resolver beside the
  governor, written once, so nobody can re-scope a proposal after votes are cast.

The split is clean: **the caveat scopes, the governor counts.** And it holds
live, on the unmodified governor. A holds 2 tokens and hands `treasury` to B,
who holds 2. A vote on `grants` (under `treasury`) counts 4. The same vote on
`community` counts 2.

## Anchored at the protocol level, not in an app

The scope of a delegation isn't stored in my front end, or in a database I run.
It lives in two places nobody owns:

- **a signed ERC-7710 delegation**, a standard, which brings revocation, chains
  and narrowing with it;
- **the Intuition graph**, where the DAO curates its own organisation, and where
  the curator's stake is what makes an edge count.

So any interface, any wallet able to redeem a delegation can use it; my app is
just one window onto it. Every voice cast in someone's name is logged on-chain
under that person, so a delegator can audit their delegate with one query
instead of trusting a dashboard. Delegating by domain is only worth it if you
stay accountable, and that has to be readable from the chain.

It also travels. Atom ids on Intuition are deterministic and chain-independent,
so the domain vocabulary seeded on testnet keeps its ids on mainnet. And *whose*
stake counts is a dial in the signed terms: a DAO's curator address for
institutional curation, or the delegator themself, so each level of a chain
reads the graph through its own signer's view of the organisation.

## What a DAO would need to adopt it

Being straight about what's left between a POC and a real DAO:

- deploy the governor extension and the domain resolver next to its voting
  token (a DAO with a governor already live would migrate or upgrade to it);
- describe its domains in the Intuition graph, and stake on the edges it
  endorses;
- decide what an attestation means. `> 0` stake is a POC test; production wants
  a ratio against the counter-triple, or a quorum of curators, and that number is
  a governance decision, not mine.

The resolver is designed to be swappable: domain in calldata, a registry, or an
adapter for a specific governor, pinned in the signed terms so nobody can
re-map a domain after the fact. That's what lets the same mechanism fit DAOs
that are organised very differently.

## Where it bit

The counting rules were where the honest work was:

- **A voice is spent once.** Delegating by domain moves no token, so without
  bookkeeping a delegator could take a domain back after their delegate voted,
  and vote again with the same weight.
- **One signature per voice carried.** My first version bundled B's own voice and
  the one B relayed from A into one signed object. When A revoked, C lost
  *both*, including what B had given on their own. Fixed by signing one
  delegation per voice.

And the last stretch was me attacking my own repo. The commit log from those days
reads like a confession: `castVoteFor` trusted whoever called it; anyone could
pick which electorate decided a proposal; the production build shipped all four
private keys; the faucet could be drained. All fixed, and a `verify` script now
replays the four closed attacks against the deployed contracts before every demo.

## What I take from it

Two things. A constraint read early (no reverse index) saves weeks of building
the wrong thing. And building on someone else's governor instead of mine
changed what I was making: not a demo that works in its own little world, but a
piece other DAOs could actually pick up.

It's the same delegation thread as [Hourglass](/projects/ourglass), pointed at
voting, and the same idea running through [Sofia](/projects/sofia) and
[ARP](/projects/arp): a knowledge graph as the source of authority, read
on-chain, and stake as the signal that someone stands behind a claim.
